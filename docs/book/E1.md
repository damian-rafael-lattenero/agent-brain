# E1 · recall, MRR, nDCG and the gate

**Denotes.** Four numbers that grade a ranking system: did the right note come first (recall@1), how high on average (MRR), how good is the whole top list (nDCG@5) — and the bar they must clear (the gate).

## 1 · The aha

One number lies less than an impression but still lies alone: recall@1 hides a bad second rank, MRR hides a bad tail, nDCG sees the whole top but not the misses below it. The gate turns the numbers into a verdict: the suite passes only when the share of cases that cleared their bar clears the suite's bar.

## 2 · What it denotes

A case asks a question and names its expected note; the run asks the store with `k = 5` and reads the rank of the expected uri: rank@5. From all cases: recall@1 (the share ranked first), MRR (the mean of one over rank), recall@5 and nDCG@5 (position-discounted gain over the top five). The gate: a suite passes when passed/total clears the declared bar — 0.95 for the retrieval suite this fleet lives on.

The numbers are computed once per run in `agent-brain/core/src/main/kotlin/dev/lattenero/brain/eval/RetrievalMetrics.kt`, and the report's markdown prints them with latency, tokens and cost — the same table the artifacts carry.

## 3 · In your languages

**Kotlin.** The metrics are a pure function over the case results: ranks in, numbers out, no store read. Full row: `kotlin-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `type-classes`.

**Nushell.** The same math by hand on the report JSON: `from json | get cases | ...` — worth doing once to see MRR is a mean, not magic. Full row: `nushell-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `type-classes`.
