# H05 · Adjunction: why currying works in both directions

**Denotes.** A pair of functors going opposite ways, tied by a unit and a counit whose two round trips are identity on the outer legs — the unique-factorizing pattern of H04, held for every object at once.

## 1 · The aha

Currying is the everyday case: `z × a → b` corresponds to `z → (a ⇒ b)`, one correspondence for ALL objects, with `eval` as the counit. B04 taught the move; H05 names what makes it lawful in both directions — and why the round trips are only half identities.

## 2 · What it denotes

Functors `L ⊣ R` with unit `η : I_D → R ∘ L` and counit `ε : L ∘ R → I_C`, obeying `ε_{L d} ∘ L η_d = id` and `R ε_c ∘ η_{R c} = id` — the hom-set form is `C(L d, c) ≅ D(d, R c)`, "natural both in `d` and `c`".

In Haskell: `unit :: a -> u (f a)`, `counit :: f (u a) -> a`, and `curry :: ((a, b) -> c) -> (a -> b -> c)` is the free ⊣ forgetful-flavored instance of the pattern — B04's move, now with its unit.

"Unit is known as `return` (or `pure`)" and "counit as `extract`"; "if a functor has an adjoint, this adjoint is unique up to isomorphism". The encoding approximates: monads "only rarely" factor into adjoint pairs "because those functors would normally take us out of `Hask`", and the Typeclassopedia has no `Adjunction` section at all. The trap from the note: a round trip through the free monoid does not return what it took — "in a free monoid, `2 * 3` is not `6` — it's a new element `[2, 3]`". Full theory: `theory-kb/notes/category-theory/adjunction.md`.
