# B09 · Recursion and folds

**Denotes.** A fold is recursion written once: the edge case becomes the starting value, the step becomes the combining function, and the shape carries the walk. The everyday loop replacement, with an order.

## 1 · The aha

A hand-written loop keeps its accumulator in a slot and its order in nobody's head. A fold puts the accumulator in the signature and the order in the name — the shape walks itself, and the combining step is the only thing you write.

## 2 · What it denotes

In Haskell: `foldr` works from the END — it can end after one constructor on a list that never ends; `foldl'` works from the START, strictly, the accumulator evaluated at every step. `RecursionFolds.hs` carries this as an executable law run, and the note's rule: lists fold, trees recurse, and the order you pick is the order your step needs.

## 3 · In your languages

**Kotlin.** `fold` is the left fold and the everyday one; `foldRight` materializes the whole list first — the order matters when the step is not symmetric (string building, running differences). A typed nothing comes home as `null` from the fold's identity. Full row: `kotlin-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `recursion-folds`.

**Nushell.** `reduce --fold 1` — the identity makes it total over empty input (the math-sum raising pitfall gone); the accumulator flows down the list, order fixed. Full row: `nushell-kb/notes/canon-map.md`.
