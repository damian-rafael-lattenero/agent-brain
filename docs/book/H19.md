# H19 · Optics: lenses, prisms and traversals as one idea

**Denotes.** Data accessors that read and write one component of a structure and are first-class, composable values — lenses for fields, prisms for variants, traversals for elements, one idea underneath.

## 1 · The aha

Every `get`/`update` pair you have written is half of one arrow: a coalgebra `a → Store s a`, with `Store s a = Store (s -> a) s` — a comonad (H16) carrying the focus and the rest. First-class means the accessor composes: one for the part, one for the whole that contains it, glued like any other arrow (H01).

## 2 · What it denotes

A lens on a whole `a` with focus `s`: `get : a → s` and `set : a → s → a`; as one arrow, `coalg_s : a → Store s a`, whose first coherence condition "translates to" the law GETPUT: `set a (get a) = a`.

The kinds answer different shapes — "the fields of a record, the variants of a union, or the elements of a container" — and the framework behind all of them is "based around the notion of profunctors, a flexible generalization of functions" (H15); "`Traversable` forms a core component" of the Haskell library, and the Yoneda embedding (H12) "provides a very useful representation" of the same accessors.

The approximation: the mirrored manuals give the get/set pair on one focus only — prisms and traversals need the representation no manual carries.

The trap: a `set` that rebuilds the whole from the part drops every other field, so `set a (get a)` is not `a` — "this is the lens law that expresses the fact that if you set a field of the structure `a` to its previous value, nothing changes". Full theory: `theory-kb/notes/category-theory/optics.md`.
