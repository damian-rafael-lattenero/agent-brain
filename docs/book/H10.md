# H10 · Free and forgetful functors: syntax against interpreter

**Denotes.** A forgetful functor drops structure and its left companion builds the structure that imposes nothing — the free object — from which a structure-preserving map is exactly a plain map of generators.

## 1 · The aha

B23 ran the free monad and tagless final as code; H10 is the shape underneath: the syntax functor builds the object that imposes nothing, and every interpreter out of it is just one function on generators — "Mon(F x, m) ≅ Set(x, U m)".

## 2 · What it denotes

The forgetful `U : Mon → Set` "maps monoids to their underlying sets"; the free `F` is "defined as the left adjoint to a forgetful functor", with `C(F x, c) ≅ D(x, U c)` natural in both — every homomorphism `F x → m` is exactly one function `x → U m`, and conversely. A free object "has no structure other than what's generated by unit and associativity laws": "instead of multiplying `2` and `3` they remember `2` and `3` in a list".

In Haskell: `data Free f a = Pure a | Free (f (Free f a))`, the syntax as one functor (`Puts`, `Gets`, `Exit`), and one `interp :: IOFree a -> IO a` — "collapse our IOFree DSL into IO monad actions"; tagless final is the other route, `class Expr rep where`.

The approximation: "in Haskell, we cannot express monoidal laws, but the following is a decent approximation of the resulting free functor". The trap: free does not mean cheap — a program expecting `6` from the free monoid gets `[2, 3]`, and nothing collapses the two until an interpreter says so. Full theory: `theory-kb/notes/category-theory/free-forgetful.md`.

## 3 · In your languages

**Kotlin.** The DSL half is documented: functions with a `Raise<E>` receiver "can be transformed into a variety of types; not only `Either` and `Ior`, but also `Result`, `Option`, or a nullable type" — one program, several interpreters, which is the tagless-final reading. Full row: `kotlin-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `free-monad-tagless-final`.

**Nushell.** Closures ARE the deferred syntax: "closures are used in Nu extensively as parameters to iteration style commands like `each`, `where`, and `reduce`", invoked later "using the `do` command" — build now, interpret later. A general free structure has no documented instance: the honest ceiling the drills work around. Full row: `nushell-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `free-monad-tagless-final`.
