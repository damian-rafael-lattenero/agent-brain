# H18 · Sheaf: local views that agree globally

**Denotes.** Data attached to the regions of a space, consistent on overlaps, such that local pieces that agree glue into one global piece.

## 1 · The aha

Every distributed system you run lives on this question: two services each hold a local view; are their views consistent on the overlap? The condition is the definition — local pieces that agree on overlaps ARE one global section, and when they do not, no global picture exists at that quality.

## 2 · What it denotes

Regions with inclusions form a category (a preorder; H01 again); a presheaf assigns each region its local data — "contravariant functors from any category `C` to `Set`" (H14's named consequence); the sheaf condition: local data that agree on overlaps have one global section. Limits of such functors are cheap — the functor category "has all small limits and colimits, and they are computed pointwise".

No Haskell type names one: the nearest thing the manuals carry is the presheaf on the category of types itself, `newtype Op r a = Op (a -> r)`, and "a presheaf is not yet a sheaf" — the gluing condition is not in any mirrored manual.

The trap: assuming views that are right on their own are consistent together — "models of specific phenomena fail when multiple sensor types are combined into a complex network, because they cannot assemble a global picture consistently", and the consistency radius "places a lower bound on the amount of distortion incurred by the fusion process". In the wild: distributed tasks where "terminating solutions are precisely its global sections", and agent state read as a copresheaf with provenance as the category of elements. Full theory: `theory-kb/notes/category-theory/sheaf.md`.

## 3 · In your languages

**Kotlin.** No documented instance in the pages read — the honest gap; the closest everyday shape is a merge of two local states, which is exactly the shape the condition warns about. Full row: `kotlin-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `denotational-design`.

**Nushell.** Overlap is resolved by overwrite, not by agreement: `merge` — "merge the input with a record or table, overwriting values in matching columns" — no agreement check exists in the language (no hit across the manual mirrors); the check is yours to write, which is what the drills practice.
