# B08 · Pattern matching and exhaustiveness

**Denotes.** One clause per shape of the input, the payloads bound by the shape itself — and the checker proving no shape was forgotten. The dispatch half of the sum-type law (B06), read from the consuming side.

## 1 · The aha

A dispatch without coverage proof is a bet that the arms and the type agree forever. The exhaustiveness check turns that bet into a compile error: add a constructor, and every unfinished dispatch lights up as a checklist item.

## 2 · What it denotes

In Haskell, `case` matches STRUCTURE: `Just (x:xs)` binds the parts as it matches, one clause per constructor, and GHC's `-Wall` reports the missing ones. The clauses ARE the function — pattern matching is definition, not lookup.

## 3 · In your languages

**Kotlin.** The `when` as an EXPRESSION over a sealed tree: exhaustive by the type checker, no `else` (B06's law deepened — as a STATEMENT the when loses the proof). Structural binding goes through data-class components in the branches. Full row: `kotlin-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `pattern-matching`.

**Nushell.** `match` compares literal arms over the tag — no structural patterns, no checker proof: a missing arm answers NOTHING, silently. The discipline: arms stay explicit (no catch-all), and the census test over every constructor is the coverage proof. Full row: `nushell-kb/notes/canon-map.md`.
