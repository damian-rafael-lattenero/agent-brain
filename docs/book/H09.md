# H09 · F-algebra and catamorphism: every fold is one equation

**Denotes.** An algebra for an endofunctor is a carrier with an evaluator that folds one layer; the initial one receives exactly one homomorphism from every other — and that unique map is the catamorphism, the general fold.

## 1 · The aha

B09 taught folds as recursion patterns; H09 says what a fold IS: the unique solution of `m = alg . fmap m . unFix`. The two arguments you always pass a fold — the combine and the starting value — are the two components of an algebra, which is why `reduce --fold` in nu feels exactly right.

## 2 · What it denotes

For an endofunctor `F`: an algebra `(a, f)` with `f : F a → a`; a homomorphism `m : (a, f) → (b, g)` is `g ∘ F m = m ∘ f`; the initial algebra `(i, j)` receives exactly one homomorphism from every algebra — Lambek's theorem says `j : F i → i` is an isomorphism. In Haskell: `newtype Fix f = Fix (f (Fix f))`, and `cata alg = alg . fmap (cata alg) . unFix` — "`foldr` is just a convenient specialization to lists", its two arguments being exactly the algebra's two components.

The honest limit: `Foldable` is weaker — it forgets the shape, and "consider trying to compute the depth of a `Tree`: try as we might, there is no way to implement it using `Foldable`". The trap: a fold is not a loop with an argument — `foldr f acc (x:xs) = f x (foldr f acc xs)` passes the recursive call to `f`, so it can terminate on an infinite list "provided `f` is lazy in its right argument". Full theory: `theory-kb/notes/category-theory/f-algebra.md`.

## 3 · In your languages

**Kotlin.** Aggregation with the algebra's two components visible: Arrow's `mapOrAccumulate` "applies the same computation uniformly to elements of an `Iterable`" — combine and accumulate; Flow's collector "consumes the transformed values". Full row: `kotlin-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `recursion-folds`.

**Nushell.** `reduce` — "aggregate a list (starting from the left) to a single value using an accumulator closure", with `--fold` for the initial value: function and starting value, the two components of the algebra, side by side in the signature. No shape-aware fold exists in the language: the honest ceiling this unit's `reduce`-family lives under. Full row: `nushell-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `recursion-folds`.
