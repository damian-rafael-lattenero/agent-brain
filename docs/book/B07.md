# B07 · Maybe and Either

**Denotes.** Absence is a constructor (`Maybe`/`T?`), failure-with-a-reason is a second shape (`Either`/a sealed pair) — the two meanings are two SHAPES the reader must tell apart, never a sentinel value wearing a costume. The measured #4 violated concept in the fleet.

## 1 · The aha

`""` standing for "no turn" makes one type carry two meanings, and only memory keeps them apart. A typed nothing (`String?`, `Maybe`) makes the absence a SHAPE — the reader consults it, the reader-side check proves the reading.

## 2 · What it denotes

In Haskell: `Maybe a` has two constructors, `Just a` and `Nothing`, and a function that may find nothing returns `Maybe` — never `""` or `-1` standing in. `Either e a` carries the reason: `Left why` refused, `Right it` worked. Construction and matching are one operation read in two directions; the empty case is a VALUE like any other.

## 3 · In your languages

**Kotlin.** `T?` is `Maybe` already: the elvis and `?.let` are the binds, `null` is `Nothing`. The law: keep the typed nothing through the whole computation and translate ONCE at the wire edge (`put("turn", turnId ?: "")` — the empty literal exists only IN the wire). A two-shape answer is a sealed sum: `Plan.Ready(path) / Plan.Refused(why)` — never `FilePlan(null, msg)`. A value class (`TurnId`) makes ids lie-proof. Full row: `kotlin-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `maybe-either`.

**Nushell.** Absence IS `null`, and the optional reads are the binds: `get -o`, `default`, `?` cell paths. The law: absence stays null — `''` is a VALUE (a deliberately empty title), and `is-empty` cannot tell the two apart; `describe` is the honest probe (`'nothing'` is the absence's name). Full row: `nushell-kb/notes/canon-map.md`.
