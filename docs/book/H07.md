# H07 · Monoid object: what a monad buys

**Denotes.** An object of a monoidal category with a multiplication from its tensor square and a unit from the tensor unit, obeying associativity and unit laws up to the category's isomorphisms.

## 1 · The aha

B14 built the monoid you fold with; this is the same picture one level up, and the slogan is the payoff: "All told, monad is just a monoid in the category of endofunctors." Swap the tensor for functor composition and the same two arrows, `μ : T ∘ T → T` and `η : I → T`, are the monad — "the laws are exactly the monad laws".

## 2 · What it denotes

A monoidal category `(C, ⊗, i)` and an object `m` with `μ : m ⊗ m → m` and `η : i → m`, associativity and unit holding through the associator and unitors. In Haskell's pair tensor: `mu . bimap id mu . alpha = mu . bimap mu id`, with the unit laws through `lambda` and `rho`.

In the endofunctor category — "endofunctors form a strict monoidal category with functor composition as tensor product" — that object IS the monad: `return >=> g = g`, `g >=> return = g`, `(g >=> h) >=> k = g >=> (h >=> k)` — "the laws simply state that `return` is the identity of `(>=>)`, and that `(>=>)` is associative".

What the slogan buys: one reading for two classes. The approximation: a monad's monoid object lives in the functor category, not in the category of types; and `Monad` is written with bind, with no transformation named. Full theory: `theory-kb/notes/category-theory/monoid-object.md`.
