# C6 · The journey of a question through the RAG

**Denotes.** What happens between typing a question and holding five passages with their sources: one vector, two ranked lists, one fused order, one confidence test.

## 1 · The aha

The store never answers. It ranks. A question becomes a vector, is compared against every passage, is compared again as words, the two orders are fused, and the caller receives the top few with where they came from.

## 2 · What it denotes

Retrieval is ranking under a budget: too few passages and the answer lacks its source, too many and the prompt drowns. Hybrid search runs two independent signals because they fail in opposite ways — the vector leg matches meaning and misses exact names, the full-text leg matches words and misses paraphrase.

RRF fuses two rankings without pretending their scores are comparable: each passage earns one divided by sixty plus its rank, list by list, so a passage near the top of both wins. A citation is the provenance that makes the passage checkable.

## 3 · In your languages

Kotlin: the fusion rule is a pure function, `Rrf.fuse(legs)` in `agent-brain/core/src/main/kotlin/dev/lattenero/brain/rag/Rrf.kt`, with the constant and the curated vote written next to it; the SQL that actually runs is its executable twin.

Nushell: the same hits become a table — `hit-rows` in `Scripts/lib/brain.nu` rounds the score to three decimals and cuts each passage at seventy characters, which is a projection of the answer, never the answer.

The difference: Kotlin fuses, Nushell renders. The snag: two copies of one rule pull apart unless a test pins them together, which is why the SQL carries a comment naming its Kotlin twin.

## 4 · In your system

The journey, with the file at each step:

1. The door: `agent-brain/core/src/main/kotlin/dev/lattenero/brain/http/Server.kt` accepts the question on its query route and hands it to the service.
2. The legs: `agent-brain/core/src/main/kotlin/dev/lattenero/brain/rag/QueryExpansion.kt` decides what to search — the question itself, an English restatement when the question arrives in another language, and a fixed set of synonyms.
3. The vector: every leg becomes 768 numbers through nomic-embed-text, the `embed_dim` of `agent-brain/core/src/main/kotlin/dev/lattenero/brain/config/Config.kt`.
4. The two lists: `agent-brain/core/src/main/sqldelight/dev/lattenero/brain/db/sq/Rag.sq` ranks passages twice — cosine over pgvector, and full text over a generated tsvector scored by cover density. Both legs filter by embedding model, so a mixed table can never rank apples against oranges. The vector leg is an exact scan today: the one-row parameters hide the constant from the HNSW planner, and exact beats approximate until the corpus outgrows it.
5. The pitfall: `websearch_to_tsquery('simple', ...)` ANDs every word, stopwords included. A natural question — "what is the difference between..." — carries words no note contains, so that leg returns nothing and only the vector leg votes.
6. The fusion: the same statement sums one over sixty plus the rank for each list a passage appears in, then adds one extra first-place vote when the uri comes from a curated notes tree (`agent-brain/core/src/main/kotlin/dev/lattenero/brain/rag/Curated.kt`).
7. The confidence test: the top passage's cosine similarity decides whether anything was served — `CONFIDENCE_FLOOR` 0.55 in `agent-brain/core/src/main/kotlin/dev/lattenero/brain/rag/RagService.kt`, calibrated on live queries.
8. The passages: the top k, each with uri, title, heading and ordinal.
9. The prompt: the caller places those passages in its prompt and the model writes the answer; the brain never writes it.

## 5 · Paper sheet

            relevant iff the top similarity is at least 0.55
drawing:    question --> vector --> {cosine, full text} --> RRF --> top k with citations

## 7 · Retrieval questions

1. Why does the store rank instead of answering, and who composes the answer?
2. What does each leg catch that the other misses, and what does RRF refuse to compare?
3. Which kind of word can switch the full-text leg off, and how do you see that it happened?

## 8 · Close-out

## Minimum path

- **The aha**: a question becomes a vector, then two ranked lists, then one fused order, then a threshold.
- **The signature**: `query : Text -> Hit*`.
- **The checkpoint**: the natural question has no full-text row; the keyword query has them.
