# H13 · Kan extension: the most general way to move a construction

**Denotes.** The best approximation of a functor along another: a universal functor on the larger domain that agrees with the original where it was defined — the left and right versions being the two companions of precomposition.

## 1 · The aha

H04 factorized one object; H13 factorizes a WHOLE functor along a mapping, universally, and the saying holds that "all concepts are Kan extensions" — limits, colimits, adjunctions, monads and the Yoneda lemma all fall out of this one construction.

## 2 · What it denotes

Functors `K : I → A` and `D : I → C`. The left extension `Lan_K D : A → C` carries a unit `η : D → Lan_K D ∘ K`, universal among such.

The right one carries `ε : Ran_K D ∘ K → D`, and every `F'` with `ε' : F' ∘ K → D` gets a unique `σ : F' → Ran_K D` with `ε' = ε · (σ ∘ K)` — `[A, C](Lan_K D, F') ≅ [I, C](D, F' ∘ K)`, and "the left Kan extension is the left adjoint, and the right Kan extension is the right adjoint of the precomposition with `K`".

In Haskell the ends become quantifiers: `newtype Ran k d a = Ran (forall i. (a -> k i) -> d i)`, `data Lan k d a = forall i. Lan (k i -> a) (d i)` — and the free functor is `data FreeF f a = forall i. FMap (i -> a) (f i)`, which "fakes the lifting of a function by recording both the function and its argument".

The approximation: "in Haskell, we cannot express monoidal laws, but the following is a decent approximation"; the mirrored manuals have no text on it. The trap: expecting `Lan_K D ∘ K = D` — the definition gives one universal arrow, not an equality, and for the right version the arrow points the other way. Full theory: `theory-kb/notes/category-theory/kan-extension.md`.
