# B15 · Foldable and Traversable

**Denotes.** Two walks over a shape: the Foldable collapses it to one value (length, sum), the Traversable walks it running a fallible step and answers the shape INSIDE the step answer — all-or-nothing.

## 1 · The aha

`map` promises the same shape; a traversable walk promises the same shape INSIDE the result of a step that can fail. The difference from a fold is exactly that twist: the collapse loses the shape, the walk preserves it across the step answer.

## 2 · What it denotes

In Haskell: `class Foldable t` gives `foldMap`/`foldr` (the collapse), `class (Functor t, Foldable t) => Traversable t` gives `traverse :: Applicative f => (a -> f b) -> t a -> f (t b)` — the order swap, pinned by the executable law runs (`FoldableTraversable.hs`). The B12 ruling is structural here: traverse is applicative, so accumulation and all-or-nothing are its native modes.
