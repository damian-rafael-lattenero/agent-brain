# H16 · Comonad: a computation that depends on its context

**Denotes.** The dual of a monad: a type constructor that comes pre-filled with context, with a way to read the value in focus and a way to duplicate the whole context around every value.

## 1 · The aha

A monad wraps values you put in; a comonad arrives with its surroundings already there. `extract` reads the focused value, `duplicate` gives "a stream of streams, each focused on a different element" — and a co-Kleisli arrow (H08, reversed) is a computation with access to everything around the focus.

## 2 · What it denotes

An endofunctor `W` with `ε : W → I` (extract) and `δ : W → W²` (duplicate) — "comonad laws are the mirror image of monad laws" — and "the comonad is a comonoid in the category of endofunctors", H07 with every arrow turned around.

In Haskell: `class Functor w => Comonad w where extract :: w a -> a; duplicate :: w a -> w (w a); extend :: (w a -> b) -> w a -> w b`, with `extend f = fmap f . duplicate` — "`extract` is the dual of `return`, `duplicate` is the dual of `join`, and `extend` is the dual of `(=<<)`".

The stream case: "'extend' computes a new Stream from an old, where the element at position n is computed as a function of everything from position n onwards" — "a co-Kleisli arrow for a stream is just a digital filter, and `extend` produces a filtered stream". The approximation: the class "is not actually in the standard Haskell libraries", and the Canon's streaming chapter does not mention it. The trap: asking it for `return` — "a comonad ... does not give the means to insert values": no way in, only out. Full theory: `theory-kb/notes/category-theory/comonad.md`.
