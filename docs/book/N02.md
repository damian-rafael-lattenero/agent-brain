# N02 · `currying.nu` — partial application as a verb

**Denotes.** Currying built where the language has none: one closure step per argument, partial applications as VALUES, records filled field by field from deferred closures.

## 1 · The aha

Nushell has no native currying, so the module builds it exactly the way kap does: `curry2`…`curry23` (the ladder is GENERATED by `nushell-fp/scripts/gen-curry.nu` — Kotlin's 23-argument ceiling, mirrored), `uncurry2`…`uncurry23` with round-trip laws pinned, and `papply` to fix leading args (kap `.with`, by value).

## 2 · What it denotes

The verbs: `curryN`/`uncurryN` (the generated ladders), `papply` (fix leading args), `apply-args` (apply a curried chain from a list, one `do` per rung; a short list IS the partial), `build-record` / `build-record-par` (fill a record from 0-arg closures — the parallel one re-raises failures AFTER the stream, naming every failed field), `thread` (left-to-right pipeline, the `>>>`). Verified: `papply` a three-arg add twice, then apply `5` — `10`.

## 3 · In your languages

**Nushell.** This module — closures returning closures, generated for arity 2..23, laws in `test_currying.nu`.

**Kotlin.** kap curries the same way (`@KapTypeSafe` validates while currying), and `build-record-par` is kap's applicative `.with` over a record: independent fields fill concurrently, the structure completes as results land (B12's shape, at the shell).

## 4 · In your system

- `~/Projects/nushell-fp/mods/currying.nu` — the module and its generator; `nushell-fp/tests/test_currying.nu` — the round-trip laws.
- The deploy scripts fill their summary records with `build-record`; `apply-args` drives curried chains from lists.
