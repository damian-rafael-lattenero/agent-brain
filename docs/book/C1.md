# C1 · The agent loop: what happens when you press Enter

**Denotes.** One press becomes a cycle: your text joins the session's context, the model answers or commands a step, the step's result rides back as input, and the cycle repeats until the final text stands.

## 1 · The aha

There is no magic in the loop — it is a `while` with a judgment in the middle. The model sees everything the session has said so far, and every step it takes becomes context for the next one, which is why order and cost both grow with the conversation.

## 2 · What it denotes

The cycle, one lap: your text enters as a user message. The model either answers (the lap ends) or issues a command — a Read, an Edit, a shell run. The harness runs it, wraps the result, and hands it back as a new input. Repeat. The fork's lap lives in `ZCode/apps/zcode-cli/packages/core/src/runtime/methods/turn.ts`.

Two facts make the loop expensive and interesting at once: every lap re-reads the whole session (the context window is a budget, A0.4), and every step is a part in the store — the session's sqlite records each one, which the C7 card later replays.

## 3 · In your languages

**Kotlin.** The bot mirrors the loop from outside: the daemon sends the prompt, waits for the frame, and mirrors the result to Telegram — the same cycle, seen from the phone side. Full row: `kotlin-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `pure-function` (the loop's steps are effects; the judgment stays pure).

**Nushell.** Your own scripts run the same shape by hand: `complete` wraps a command's result as a value, and the next statement decides from it — one lap of the loop, written down. Full row: `nushell-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `composition`.

## 4 · In your system

- `ZCode/apps/zcode-cli/packages/core/src/runtime/methods/turn.ts` — the lap as the fork runs it.
