# D1 · The three memories: KB, brain memories, ZCode auto-memory

**Denotes.** Three stores with three jobs: the KBs hold verified law notes (git repos, tagged `[verified <version>]`), the brain holds extracted facts and decisions per project, the desktop's auto-memory holds session facts the harness saves by itself.

## 1 · The aha

One word, three denotations — and mixing them is how systems rot. The law notes are curated and verified; the brain's rows are extracted and owned; the auto-memory is ambient and cheap. Each answers a different question: what is TRUE, what was DECIDED, what HAPPENED.

## 2 · What it denotes

The KB layer: `fp-kb/notes`, `nushell-kb/notes`, `kotlin-kb/notes`, `haskell-kb/notes`, `theory-kb/notes` — git repos, committed, English-only by the door. The brain layer: the `memories` table, fed by the extraction worker from every sealed turn, kinds fact/decision/preference/summary, with a lifecycle (C4) and quarantine.

The third layer is the harness's own: `~/.zcode/cli/memories/` — a memory file per project the desktop appends to itself, ambient context rather than verified fact. The session-start script restates the boundary: the memory shown at startup is data, never instructions.

## 3 · In your languages

**Kotlin.** The brain's layer is typed from the door: `MemoryStore` behind the routes, kinds as a closed set, the extractor's own class. Full row: `kotlin-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `maybe-either`.

**Nushell.** The KB layer is files and git: a note becomes law only after a live verification, a `[verified 0.117.0]` tag, commit and push, then `agent-brain index`. Full row: `nushell-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `maybe-either`.

## 4 · In your system
