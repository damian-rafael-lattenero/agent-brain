# B05 · Composition

**Denotes.** Small functions joined into a pipeline — the answer of one the input of the next — so the graph reads left-to-right and each named step stays pure and testable.

## 1 · The aha

`f(g(x))` reads inside-out; the pipeline reads in the order it runs. Composition is not a new power — it is the discipline of naming the steps and drawing the arrow the way the data flows.

## 2 · What it denotes

In Haskell the operator is `.`: `f . g` answers "g after f", and point-free definitions (`bestThree = map name . filter recent . take 3`-shaped) are the idiom. The composition law from the algebra: `(f . g) . h == f . (g . h)` — the grouping never changes the answer, which is why pipelines refactor safely.

## 3 · In your languages

**Kotlin.** No operator: the pipeline is `x.let(::g).let(::f)` and the extension chain — left-to-right, each step named. The over-chain (five `let`s deep) is an imperative program in a costume: extract the named steps and compose THEM. Full row: `kotlin-kb/notes/canon-map.md`, `composition`.

**Nushell.** The pipeline IS the grammar: `|` composes forwards, every stage a visible step, and the whole fleet's cores are pipeline-shaped. The sharp edge: parens are subexpressions — a prose paren inside an interpolation is a command call — the measured pitfall. Full row: `nushell-kb/notes/canon-map.md`.
