## 2 · What it denotes

Read 2026-11-26, mostly as abstract pages, and this field "moves fastest of all, so re-read every row before relying on it".

The landings: a new foundation "building on ideas in pure mathematics relating to categories and functors" (Mahadevan); gradient descent "defines a monoidal functor from a category of parametrised functions to this category of update rules" (Fong, Spivak and Tuyeras — H06); "a purely diagrammatic calculus which exposes how the information flows between the words in a sentence" (Coecke et al. — H17); agent memory "transported by the left Kan extension `Lan_u I_t`" (Buehler — H13); and the negatives: "three negative results on consistency-style categorical priors" (Kari et al. — H18 among them).

Formal proofs live in Lean, not Haskell: LeanCat's benchmark has "100 fully formalized category-theory tasks", where "the best state-of-the-art model solves only 12.0% of tasks at pass@4" — "a failure in compositional generalization". No Haskell type came out of these papers: the manuals have no text on learning, and a library would have to pick instances none ships.

The trap: taking a categorical title for a working system — the shape being right does not make the loss term, or the model, work. Full theory: `theory-kb/notes/category-theory/category-theory-and-ai.md`.
