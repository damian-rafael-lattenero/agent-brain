# N07 · `lazy.nu` — deferring work as a value

**Denotes.** The defer law's mechanisms in a strict shell: closures as thunks, short-circuit as the free laziness, streams as the lazy pipeline.

## 1 · The aha

Nushell is EAGER: `let x = expensive` evaluates NOW — no thunks at binding time, no lazy lists. What the shell HAS (measured live, 0.117): closures are thunks, `and`/`or` short-circuit (the right side skips), pipelines stream end to end (`seq 1 5000000 | first 3` in microseconds), and guards precede builds. The module formalizes the lazy-record idiom the healthcheck legs live by. THE LAW: defer expensive or conditional work in closures; force only at the point of use. ANTI-LAW: premature laziness — an always-used value builds eagerly and correctly.

## 2 · What it denotes

`thunk` (wrap a closure into the lazy record — building is free, nothing runs), `force` (run it NOW; re-force runs again — caching is the caller's policy), `force-with` (the deferred function receives its argument: a deferred CALL, not just a deferred value). Verified: `thunk {|| 7 * 6 }` built without running, `force` answers `42`.

## 3 · In your languages

**Nushell.** This module plus the three free islands (short-circuit, streaming, guard-before-build) — B16's laziness row, at the shell.

**Kotlin.** `by lazy` delegates (memoized — the shell's `force` is deliberately NOT), `sequence` generators (the streaming island), and B16's three islands name the same map: the language gives the memoized version, the shell gives the raw thunk and leaves the policy to you.
