# H17 · Monoidal category and string diagrams: wiring that composes

**Denotes.** A category with a tensor that is a bifunctor and a unit, associative and unital only up to natural isomorphism — which lets boxes and wires stand for composite morphisms, read "as a syntax with its semantics".

## 1 · The aha

H06 used the tensor; H17 names the stage it stands on. The picture is the payoff: composition is wiring boxes in series, the tensor is placing them side by side, one wire per object, the unit is no wire — and every equation you have written since H06 becomes a picture you can rewire.

## 2 · What it denotes

A category `C` with a bifunctor `⊗ : C × C → C` and a unit `i`, plus the associator `α_{a,b,c} : (a ⊗ b) ⊗ c → a ⊗ (b ⊗ c)` and the two unitors `λ`, `ρ` — natural isomorphisms that "must satisfy coherence conditions"; symmetric adds `γ_{a,b} : a ⊗ b → b ⊗ a`. In Haskell, the manual's own classes carry the pieces: `bimap`, `associate`, `idl`, `idr`, `braid` — and the Canon lands on `Applicative`, "that of a lax monoidal functor" (H06).

The approximation is the point: "the Cartesian product is not strictly associative --- `(x, (y, z))` is not the same as `((x, y), z)`", so the isomorphisms are data, not equalities — the trap is writing `⊗` as if they were equalities: "we can't just write point-free: `mu . bimap id mu = mu . bimap mu id`". The same pictures appear across the sciences: circuits, signal flow graphs, Petri nets, Bayesian networks, neural networks — and wiring diagrams "depict systems as boxes, showing how they send signals to each other". Full theory: `theory-kb/notes/category-theory/monoidal-category.md`.
